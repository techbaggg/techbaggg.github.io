BERMUDA TRIANGLE FACTS

The Bermuda Triangle: Facts, Stories and Theories

By Jagdish Krishanlal Arora

The Bermuda Triangle is a popular name for a broad area of the western North Atlantic associated in popular culture with disappearances of ships and aircraft. Stories about the region have inspired books, documentaries and fictional explanations ranging from unusual weather to extraordinary phenomena. Separating the documented record from the mythology is the key to understanding the subject.

Where is the Bermuda Triangle?

There is no universally agreed official boundary for the Bermuda Triangle. Popular descriptions commonly place it between Florida, Bermuda and Puerto Rico, but the exact shape varies by source. That ambiguity matters because statistics about accidents depend on what geographic area is included.

Why do accidents happen at sea?

The western Atlantic contains busy shipping and aviation routes, changing weather, strong currents and periods of severe storms. Navigation errors, mechanical failures and difficult sea conditions can contribute to accidents. The ocean also makes searches and recovery difficult, particularly when incidents occur far from shore.

Where do the theories come from?

Popular theories have included magnetic anomalies, unusual weather, methane releases, extraterrestrial explanations and other ideas. Some are presented as speculative possibilities rather than established causes. Extraordinary explanations require evidence that distinguishes them from ordinary maritime and aviation risks.

Why the mystery persists

The Bermuda Triangle has become a cultural mystery partly because memorable stories are easier to repeat than complicated accident reports. The subject remains interesting as a case study in how legends develop around incomplete information.
